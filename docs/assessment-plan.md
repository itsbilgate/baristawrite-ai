# ESP Writing Assessment AI Development
## English for Baristas

## 1. Project purpose
This project develops a writing assessment for an English for Baristas ESP course. The assessment should reflect realistic written communication in a hospitality workplace and align with specific, observable learning objectives.

## 2. Source syllabus and limits
**Source:** Richards (2017), *Curriculum Development in Language Teaching*, Case Study 3: “A course in English for baristas” (pp. 53–55), as provided for this assignment.

The case describes:
- Learners at pre-intermediate level (approximately CEFR B1).
- A five-week course for international students preparing for hospitality work in Brisbane.
- General goals including context-specific vocabulary, oral and written communication, interpersonal skills, and intercultural skills.
- Use of authentic spoken and written materials, including coffee-roaster videos and industry publications/websites.
- Assessment activities that mainly replicate workplace tasks, with speaking tasks and role-plays emphasized.

**Important limitation:** The case does not provide a separate, explicit list of specific writing objectives or a dedicated writing test. The objectives below are therefore proposed objectives derived from the course's general written-communication goal and the barista workplace context. They should not be represented as verbatim objectives from Richards (2017).

## 3. Proposed writing objectives
By the end of the assessment, learners will be able to:

1. Write a clear order note that records a customer's drink, size, milk preference, and special instructions accurately.
2. Write a short, polite response to a customer complaint using appropriate service language.
3. Write a concise workplace message to a colleague about a shift change or task-related issue.
4. Write a short menu description for a coffee product using relevant vocabulary and clear descriptive language.

These objectives are measurable through written products. The first assessment prototype will focus on Objective 2 because it combines workplace authenticity, audience awareness, clarity, and appropriate tone.

## 4. Initial assessment task
### Task: Responding to a customer complaint
**Situation:** A customer sends a message saying that the oat-milk latte they ordered was prepared with regular milk. They are upset because they requested oat milk. Write a short reply as the barista.

**Candidate instructions:**
- Write 60–90 words.
- Apologize politely.
- Acknowledge the specific problem.
- Explain what you will do to resolve it.
- Use a professional and friendly tone.
- Do not blame the customer or another staff member.

**Construct assessed:** workplace writing for customer service, including task completion, clarity, organization, appropriate tone, and relevant language use.

## 5. Draft analytic rubric (20 points)
Each criterion is scored from 1 to 4. A score of 1 indicates that the criterion is not yet adequately demonstrated; 4 indicates that it is demonstrated effectively.

| Criterion | 4 - Strong | 3 - Effective | 2 - Developing | 1 - Limited |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Task completion | Addresses all required points specifically | Addresses most required points | Omits important information | Does not adequately address the complaint |
| Organization and clarity | Message is clear, logical, and easy to follow | Mostly clear and logically ordered | Some unclear or poorly ordered ideas | Difficult to understand |
| Tone and audience awareness | Polite, empathetic, and professional throughout | Generally appropriate tone | Tone is uneven or insufficiently empathetic | Tone is inappropriate for customer service |
| Language use | Vocabulary and grammar support clear communication; errors are minor | Meaning is clear despite some errors | Errors sometimes affect clarity | Frequent errors make meaning difficult to understand |
| **Total** | **20 points maximum** | | | |

## 6. Alignment map
| Writing objective / construct | Evidence in the response | Rubric criteria |
|---|---|---|
| Respond politely to a customer complaint | Apology and customer-appropriate language | Task completion; tone and audience awareness |
| Acknowledge the specific problem | Explicit reference to the incorrect milk choice | Task completion; organization and clarity |
| Explain a resolution | Clear next step, such as remaking the drink | Task completion; organization and clarity |
| Communicate professionally in writing | A concise, understandable message | Organization and clarity; tone; language use |

## 7. Development procedure
Following the six-step procedure outlined in the course PPT:

1. **Conduct professional needs analysis.** Check the written communication tasks baristas actually perform, such as order notes, customer messages, and staff messages. The present task is a draft and has not yet been validated by workplace stakeholders.
2. **Define specific communicative objectives.** Use observable outcomes such as writing a polite, clear response to a complaint.
3. **Design realistic task prompts and materials.** Present a plausible customer-service situation and clear candidate instructions.
4. **Establish criterion-referenced rubrics.** Use the analytic rubric above to score performance against stated criteria.
5. **Pilot test and refine.** Ask a teacher and a small group of learners to review the prompt, timing, difficulty, and rubric; revise based on feedback. No pilot has been conducted yet.
6. **Implement under standardized conditions.** Provide the same instructions, time limit, permitted resources, and scoring procedure to all learners.

## 8. Role of AI in this project
The AI component can help generate a task prompt from a selected objective, provide rubric-aligned feedback on a learner's draft, and explain suggested revisions. AI feedback should be treated as formative support, not as a fully validated or automatically final grade. A teacher should review high-stakes scoring and check that feedback follows the rubric.
